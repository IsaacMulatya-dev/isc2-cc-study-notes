# Domain 2: Security Governance

## 1. Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC)
* **Governance:** Establishing strategic direction, accountability structures and oversight to ensure security aligns with business objectives.
* **Risk Management:** Identifying, assessing and prioritizing organizational risks within executive-defined risk appetite and tolerance thresholds.
* **Compliance:** Adhering to applicable laws, statutory regulations, industry standards (such as ISO/IEC 27001, PCI DSS or GDPR) and contractual obligations.
* **Board and Executive Accountability:** Senior leadership owns organizational risk and approves security strategy, budget and policy frameworks.

## 2. Security Roles and Organizational Responsibilities
* **Senior Management / CISO:** Sets strategic vision, secures executive sponsorship and aligns security investments with business goals.
* **Data Owner:** Senior business leader accountable for classifying data and defining access permissions.
* **Data Custodian:** Technical administrator responsible for implementing backups, encryption and access controls per the data owner's rules.
* **Users:** Personnel obligated to follow acceptable use policies, report suspicious activity and safeguard credentials.
* **Auditors:** Independent evaluators who assess control effectiveness and regulatory compliance objectively.

## 3. Security Awareness, Training and Culture
* **Awareness:** Broad, continuous reminders that keep security top-of-mind across all employees (phishing simulations, newsletters and posters).
* **Training:** Role-specific skill-building focused on teaching personnel how to execute secure procedures.
* **Education:** Formal academic or certification programs designed for career development and deep expertise.
* **Building Security Culture:** Shifting staff from passive compliance to proactive reporting through blameless incident reporting and leadership modeling.

## 4. Measuring Security Program Effectiveness
* **Metrics and KPIs:** Key Performance Indicators measure operational control efficiency (mean time to detect, patch compliance rates or phishing click rates).
* **Key Risk Indicators (KRIs):** Early-warning signals highlighting rising risk exposure before thresholds are breached (unpatched critical vulnerabilities or spike in failed logins).
* **Dashboards and Reporting:** Translating technical telemetry into executive-ready risk summaries tailored to stakeholders, auditors and board members.

## 5. Resilience and Redundancy Concepts
* **Single Point of Failure (SPOF):** Any component whose failure halts an entire system or business process.
* **High Availability and Redundancy:** Eliminating SPOFs using clustering, load balancing, RAID storage, dual power supplies and geographic replication.
* **Business Continuity Planning (BCP):** Maintaining critical business operations during a major disruption.
* **Disaster Recovery Planning (DRP):** Restoring IT infrastructure, applications and data after a catastrophic event.
* **Recovery Metrics:**
  * **Recovery Time Objective (RTO):** Maximum tolerable downtime before business impact becomes unacceptable.
  * **Recovery Point Objective (RPO):** Maximum tolerable data loss measured backward in time from the incident.
  * **Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF):** Average operational time between hardware or system failures.
  * **Mean Time to Repair (MTTR):** Average time required to diagnose, fix and restore a failed component.

## 6. Third-Party and AI Governance
* **Vendor Risk Management:** Evaluating supplier security posture prior to onboarding through due diligence questionnaires, SOC 2 reports and Service Level Agreements (SLAs).
* **AI Governance:** Establishing acceptable use policies, human-in-the-loop oversight, bias evaluations and vendor compliance reviews for generative and predictive AI systems.
